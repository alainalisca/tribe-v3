# Tribe: Strategy Reconciliation Spec

**Purpose.** This document reconciles two things that have been running in parallel: the founding-document vision (Tribe as a promotion and storytelling engine, the three-layer model, "we make you visible") and the operational and commercial work built in the Cowork/ops chat (the two-sided marketplace mechanics, Ana's instructor outreach and social system, the data-science collaborator brief, and the accelerator application spec). It shows they are not two competing visions but two halves of one machine, and it adds strategic inputs that neither thread had on its own. Read the Founding Document first for the soul; read this for how the soul and the business fit together.

Last updated: 2026-07-31. Companion files: `Tribe_Founding_Doc_Tickets.md` (the actionable layer), `Tribe_Accelerator_Application_Spec.md`, and the Founding Document itself.

---

## 1. The reconciliation in one paragraph

The Founding Document answers **why instructors join and stay** (Tribe promotes them, it makes them visible, that is the moat). The marketplace work answers **how value gets transacted and how Tribe eventually earns** (instructors run live sessions, athletes discover and join, paid sessions flow through the platform, a fee comes later). These are the same flywheel seen from two angles. Attention is the hook, the transaction is the engine, and the stories that come out of the transaction become the content that pulls the next instructor in. Money flows through the marketplace. Loyalty flows through the promotion. Neither works alone: a marketplace with no promotion layer is "another tool competing on features," and a promotion engine with no transaction is a media hobby with no revenue. Tribe is defensible precisely because it owns both.

## 2. The unified model

The Founding Document's three layers, now integrated with the marketplace mechanics and the two-product structure already in development.

**Layer 1, the App (utility, live now).** Instructors create sessions (free or paid), athletes discover and join, real-time messaging, follow, review. This is the two-sided marketplace: instructors are supply, athletes are demand, and the platform's job is liquidity. It has to work, but on its own it is not the reason anyone stays.

**Layer 2, the Media and Promotion Engine (the differentiator, near-term, partly live already).** Tribe promotes its instructors and athletes: interviews, stories, headshots, video, and the equitable social rotation that features every instructor. This is the answer to the exact problem the notes kept circling, "give instructors a better reason to use the app." The reason is: you focus on training, we make you seen. Important reconciliation point covered in Section 4: this layer is not hypothetical. Pieces of it are already running in the ops work.

**Layer 3, Tribe.TV and the Movement (audience, long-term, parked).** The storytelling brand, sponsorships for amateur athletes, events, produced content. The media becomes the marketing and eventually its own asset. This stays parked until Layers 1 and 2 are validated.

**How the two products sit inside this.** Tribe (consumer app) is Layer 1 plus the athlete-facing side of Layer 2. Tribe.OS (instructor platform, in development) is self-serve software and business tooling for instructors. The Media and Promotion Engine is different from Tribe.OS: the engine is a done-for-you service (Tribe's team promotes the instructor), while Tribe.OS is a do-it-yourself toolset. They are complementary. The service is what makes the software worth adopting, and both are expressions of "we make you more visible and more backed." Keep Tribe+ (athlete subscription) and Tribe.OS premium (instructor subscription) distinct as before.

---

## 3. The flywheel, stated as mechanics

1. The promotion engine (Layer 2) gives an instructor a concrete reason to join and stay: Tribe makes them visible without them having to self-promote.
2. A visible instructor posts more sessions and fills them (Layer 1 marketplace liquidity). Note from the ops work: instructors demonstrably post more when the platform features them, which is direct evidence this loop is real.
3. Those sessions and the people in them generate stories and media (raw material for Layer 2 and eventually Layer 3).
4. That content attracts the next instructor and the next athlete back into Layer 1.
5. "Promote other people" is the axle the whole wheel turns on.

The commercial layer rides on top: paid sessions happen today (instructor collects directly), and the platform fee, Tribe+ subscriptions, and Tribe.OS premium are the later monetization of a wheel that is already turning.

## 4. Where this chat's work already fits (the promotion layer is partly live)

This is the most useful reconciliation, because it means Layer 2 is not a future build, it is an existing motion that now has a name and a rationale.

- **Ana's instructor reminder message already carries the promotion offer.** It asks instructors for photos and videos, promises to promote their sessions on Tribe's social channels, and tells them Tribe is building the capacity to edit and post their existing videos to TikTok and Instagram. That is Layer 2, in the field, right now.
- **The equitable social rotation calendar is the promotion engine running.** The two-week rotation that features all eight instructors evenly is literally "we make everyone visible, fairly." It is the mechanical expression of "promote other people."
- **The relationship-first outreach posture matches the doc.** The instructor messages were deliberately rewritten away from salesy software-pitching toward warm, community, "we are building this together" framing. That is the same posture the Founding Document prescribes: offer attention, not features.
- **The data-science collaborator and accelerator threads are the Layer 3 and scaling groundwork.** They are early, but they are the beginning of turning the movement and the data into assets.

Implication: the founding vision does not require Al to stop and build something new to "add the media layer." It requires formalizing and sharpening what Ana and the social system are already doing, and pulling the instructor-facing promotion offer forward as the lead pitch.

## 5. Added strategic inputs (what neither thread had alone)

**5.1 The moat play and the accelerator-readiness play are the same action.** The Founding Document names the gym partnership (BullBox, and Al's home gym Zona de Combate) as the structural moat: clustered acquisition, a whole gym's roster at once instead of one signup at a time. The accelerator spec's single biggest gap is traction slope, week-over-week movement in instructors, sessions, and paid transactions. A gym pilot is the cleanest way to produce that slope. So "prove the model with one gym" is simultaneously the moat, the validation, and the fundraising story. Do not treat these as three projects. They are one pilot.

**5.2 Lead external pitches with the promotion moat, not the marketplace.** The DS brief and accelerator spec currently lead with the marketplace and liquidity story. "Another two-sided marketplace" is a crowded, less defensible pitch. "We own the promotion and attention layer for everyday athletes and independent instructors in a region no serious competitor covers" is more defensible and more memorable. Reframe the investor and collaborator narrative to lead with the attention posture and use the marketplace as the monetization mechanism underneath it. This directly strengthens the accelerator applications already spec'd.

**5.3 The data model orientation serves the data scientist too.** The Founding Document's one endorsed code-level move now is to model people as athletes with stories and media attached, not just users and sessions. That same orientation is exactly what makes a future data-science practice valuable: story, media, engagement, and reach data are the inputs to Layer 3 sponsorship matching and to any recommendation or content-ranking work. The promotion posture and the data-science opportunity point at the same schema.

**5.4 The promotion offer has a real cost tail, so keep it lean.** The notes casually list headshots, a white background, media setup, video interviews, and editing. Per standing cost discipline: the media offer is the right thing to pull forward, but studio setup and production capacity carry real money and real time (equipment, editing hours, Ana's capacity). Start with what already works, existing instructor footage plus simple edits and the social rotation, which Ana is already spinning up, and defer any studio, gear, or headcount investment until the instructor pipeline justifies it. Do not let "offer a media team" quietly become a capital expense before there is validated demand.

**5.5 Keep the two products from blurring into the media layer.** Because Layer 2 (media service) and Tribe.OS (instructor software) both live on the instructor side, they will tend to blur. Hold the line: Tribe.OS is software the instructor operates, the promotion engine is a service Tribe performs. Conflating them makes both harder to explain and to price.

## 6. Positioning for external audiences

For investors, accelerators, and the data-science collaborator, order the story this way:

1. **Posture first:** Tribe is the home of the everyday athlete, and its niche is promoting other people. It owns attention in a market that is starving for it.
2. **Moat:** Latin America first (no serious competitor present), acquired gym by gym for clustered supply, defended by the promotion layer rather than by features.
3. **Mechanism:** a two-sided marketplace of instructors and athletes, with live sessions as the unit of transaction.
4. **Monetization:** paid sessions today (instructor-collected), then a platform fee, Tribe+ athlete subscriptions, and Tribe.OS premium.
5. **Trajectory:** the media engine becomes Tribe.TV and an athlete-sponsorship marketplace once the data and the audience exist.

This keeps the emotional and defensible parts up front and the mechanics where they belong, underneath.

## 7. The master filter

Before almost any decision, a feature, a message to an instructor, a line of copy, a schema choice, a spend, run it through one question: **does this make someone more visible, more seen, or more backed?** If yes, it is on-mission. If no, it is probably a distraction. This is "promote other people" turned into a test, and it kills scope creep automatically.
